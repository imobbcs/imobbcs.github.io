Playbook

How to Launch a Consumer Crypto App in Europe

By Imo Babics · 10 October 2026 · 9 min read

On paper, Europe is one market. On a phone screen, it's 27. This is the playbook I wish someone had handed me before my first launch: how to pick countries, what to localise and what to leave alone, where growth actually comes from, and the mistakes I keep watching well-funded teams make.

I've done this twice. At Bitpanda, as Head of Marketing, we grew from around 100,000 to over 1.1 million users. At Relai, I led the expansion from Switzerland into Germany, Italy, Spain and beyond, running growth in five languages. Monthly active users grew 10x in two years, and around 70% of this year's signups are organic.

This is written for founders and growth leads at consumer crypto and fintech companies (savings, investing, payments, neobanking) who are about to cross a European border. That could be your first one as a European scale-up, or your first one as a US, UK or Swiss company with a fresh EU licence.

01The licence gives you a market, not customers

A single EU licence lets you serve every member state. Most teams read that as "launch everywhere". It's the most expensive way to get noticed nowhere.

Europe, the UK included, has a long list of half-committed entries. Robinhood built a UK waitlist of 250,000 people, then postponed the launch in 2020 to focus on the US.1 Wealthsimple sold its UK book of around 16,000 customers in 2021 because "it's about focus".2 Public.com closed its UK operation after about eight months.3 None of these were bad products. They were short bets in a market that rewards long ones.

My rule: pick two or three countries, win them, and only add the next one when the first ones keep growing without you watching them every day.

02Pick markets with a scorecard, not a population chart

Germany is the biggest prize, so it's usually first on the slide. It's also one of the hardest places to start. Cash still settled 51% of payments in 2023,4 savers research for weeks before they move money, and strong local champions already own the trust. That doesn't mean skip it. It means know what kind of fight you're walking into.

Before you choose, score every candidate market on six questions:

CriterionThe question to ask
Investing habitHow many adults already save or invest beyond a bank account?

03Localise the plumbing, not the product

Revolut and Trade Republic look like opposites. Revolut runs 38 markets on a single app and favours a standardised approach.9 Trade Republic talks about "deeply localized offerings".10 Look closer and they've converged on the same thing.

Trade Republic's co-founder Christian Hecker advises sticking to one business model in every country, because country-specific solutions kill economies of scale.11 Yet both companies localise the layer where money moves. In 2022, Revolut moved around 2 million French customers onto French IBANs, because employers and utilities kept refusing foreign ones.12 Trade Republic rolled out national IBANs and local savings products in its biggest markets.10

One product. One brand. One engine.

Local money in, local money out, local language, local faces.

In practice, that means three things per market, before any campaign:

  • Top-ups the way locals pay. The moment someone tops up is the moment of highest intent. If the method they use every day isn't there, you lose them right there.
  • A local IBAN where it matters. Especially if you want salary deposits or direct debits.
  • Native-language support from day one. A translated help centre with an English chat queue doesn't count. People are trusting you with their savings.

04Make search the front door, in every language

Before Europeans put savings anywhere, they research. In their own language, on Google, in the app stores, and increasingly in AI assistants. If you aren't there when they look, your ads are paying to send them to someone else's search result.

That means native content per market, written for the questions locals actually ask. It means app store listings with local keywords, screenshots and reviews. And it means starting six months before you need the traffic, because search compounds slowly and then all at once.

At Relai, this is the backbone of growth: top-5 rankings in Germany and #1 in Switzerland for core Bitcoin buying and savings searches, behind only Coinbase, Kraken, Bison and Ledger, and around five times the AI-search visibility of our Bitcoin-only peers.

05Build the organic engine first. Paid comes second

Paid acquisition for crypto in Europe is expensive, and the big ad platforms limit what you can run. Creative that works in one market often falls flat in the next, so brief and test per country instead of translating one campaign.

The organic engine (search, referral, community, local press) is slower to build and much harder to copy. Irina Scarlat, Revolut's former Global Head of Growth, joined as Romania country manager at around 20,000 users and grew it towards 1.5 million. It became the first Revolut market to grow faster than the UK, with no budget.13 Her tools were community events open to non-users, active users turned into ambassadors, and one well-timed promotion (a free card for five days) that took the market from 75,000 to 100,000 users.

Her test for real product-led growth is worth stealing: people referring you without any incentive. At Relai, around 70% of 2026 signups are organic. Paid media is the accelerator, not the engine.

My rule: don't switch on paid in a new market until organic signups convert and 90-day retention looks healthy there. Paid amplifies whatever is already there, including a leaky funnel.

06Sell saving, not trading

Across the 14 European countries BlackRock surveys, around 25 million adults invest in crypto. That's 8% of the adult population.5 The other 92% don't want a trading screen. They want a plan they can set up once and trust.

That saving habit is spreading fast. ETF savings plans in continental Europe grew from 10.8 million to 15.1 million in a single year, and 68% of the next wave of ETF investors there is expected to come from outside Germany.14 Your next customers are already learning to save monthly. Meet them there.

In practice: lead with recurring buys, small amounts and long time horizons. Track recurring plans set up as your main metric. A first trade tells you very little. Repositioning Relai from a Bitcoin trading app to a Bitcoin savings app was one of the best growth decisions we made.

07Your onboarding queue is a growth ceiling

Growth that outruns operations doesn't stay growth for long. A campaign that lands well in a new market can bury a support team that doesn't speak the language, and every unanswered ticket is a review in the app store.

Signup completion rates differ by country, because the documents people sign up with differ. Support queues explode in a language nobody on the team speaks. Fraud finds new markets quickly. Brief operations before every launch and campaign, staff native-language support before the press goes out, and track onboarding pass rates per country every week.

08Put someone senior on Europe, with real decision rights

The companies winning Europe treat it as a business with its own leadership. As early as 2019, Revolut was building local growth teams in 19 European countries, backed by central teams for growth services and product.15 In 2025 it made Paris its Western European headquarters, with over 5 million French customers and more than €1 billion of planned investment.9 Trade Republic opened local branches in France, Spain and Italy, and over a third of its customers already come from outside Germany.10

The pattern: one central engine for data, product, brand and lifecycle, local voices for content, community, support and press, and one senior owner for Europe. That owner has a say in product priorities, pricing and support as well as the marketing budget. A small, AI-native team can run five languages if the system is right. Hiring country managers before the system exists multiplies cost, not growth.

Checklist: before you spend a euro on media

  • The market was picked on the scorecard above
  • The payment method locals use most is live for top-ups
  • A local IBAN is available, or you know why you don't need one
  • The app, website and help centre are written by native speakers
  • Native-language support is staffed for local working hours
  • Local search content and app store listings have been live for months, not days
  • Referral is switched on and tracked by country
  • A local press list and the first community events are planned
  • Creative is briefed and tested per country, not translated
  • Operations know the launch date and the expected volume
  • Scale, hold and exit criteria are agreed in writing
  • One senior person owns the market and can change product priorities

Europe rewards companies that commit. It punishes the ones that only visit.

None of this is a secret. It's just slower and less exciting than a big launch campaign, which is exactly why so few companies do it properly. The ones that do end up owning their category for a decade.
